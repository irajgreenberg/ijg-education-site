Creative coding → generative AI → studio pedagogy

Creative coding was never only about code. It was always about inventing a new expressive medium.

Over three decades, Ira Greenberg’s work has helped define creative coding as a bridge between art, design, and computer science. As Professor and Chair of the Center for Creative Computation at SMU in Dallas, he has helped build creative computation and creative coding pathways across graduate, undergraduate, community college, and high school contexts. IJG Labs extends that trajectory into the age of generative AI: a platform where artists, students, and non-native technologists can learn to think with code, work with AI, and build public-facing work across image, interaction, motion, and form.

The lineage

From Processing to AI-native creative computation.

2007

Processing: Creative Coding and Computational Art

Greenberg authored the first major language reference on Processing, helping establish code as an artistic medium while opening the door for artists and designers entering computation — part of a much broader teaching arc that has connected creative coding to learners ranging from first-time makers to advanced computer scientists.

2013

Processing: Creative Coding and Generative Art in Processing 2

The follow-up text translated NSF-backed teaching practice into an introductory computing framework, linking digital arts pedagogy with core CS learning outcomes.

Now

IJG Labs

That same arc now expands into generative AI, 3D, public exhibition, and Web3-native publishing: a studio-classroom-gallery ecosystem where learners create with computation rather than merely consuming automation.

The premise

The classroom does not need more synthetic speed. It needs better structures for judgment, experimentation, and reflection.

01

Code as expressive material

Creative coding always treated code as a medium for drawing, motion, emergence, structure, and play. AI expands that field rather than replacing it.

02

AI as collaborator, not shortcut

The pedagogical target is judgment, taste, iteration, and systems thinking. Learners work with AI by prompting, shaping, editing, comparing, critiquing, and reflecting.

03

Studio pedagogy over syntax drills

Students learn most deeply when they build meaningful artifacts. The classroom becomes a studio: make, inspect, revise, discuss, and exhibit.

What learners gain

  • Artists and creators without conventional technical backgrounds can enter computation through making, not gatekeeping.
  • Students learn to direct AI with taste, ethics, context, and visual literacy rather than passive dependence.
  • Creative coding remains central, now amplified by systems that can generate, explain, transform, and prototype at new scales.
  • The platform joins studio practice, curriculum scaffolding, and public exhibition into one ecosystem.

The wager

If the first wave of creative coding made computation legible to artists, the next wave must make AI legible, critiqueable, and creatively pliable.

That means teaching people how to work with models while staying grounded in form, concept, craft, ethics, and public expression. Not “learn a tool.” Not “prompt and pray.” A disciplined creative environment where artists and students build computational fluency by making work that matters.
